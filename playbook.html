Email Scripts

Email Scripts and Outreach Playbook

Frameworks, not scripts. Personalize every email with something real and specific about that business. Generic emails get ignored.

Rules, Read This First

Never skip the CAN-SPAM footer, and never re-contact anyone who unsubscribed. Every email needs "To stop receiving these emails, reply with unsubscribe," and it's a legal requirement, not a style choice. Once someone unsubscribes, that's final, permanently.

Do

  • Reference something real and specific about that business
  • Keep every email under 100 words
  • End with exactly one question
  • Log every business you research, even ones you skip
  • Notify the owner the moment a lead goes warm

Don't

  • Use the same email word-for-word on multiple businesses
  • Send from your personal email address
  • Promise a specific price or timeline
  • Argue with anyone who says not interested
  • Contact anyone who unsubscribed, ever again
  • Send more than 40 emails per day per inbox
  • Skip the CAN-SPAM footer

Research Every Business First

Take the time this actually requires. Answer the questions below with something specific to that business before you write the email, generic openers get ignored. If you can't find anything specific after looking, it's fine to skip the lead.

  1. What exactly does this business do?
  2. Do they have a website? If yes, is it actually outdated?
  3. Are they on Google Maps? Do they have reviews?
  4. Facebook page but no website?
  5. Can you find the owner's name anywhere?
  6. Anything specific worth mentioning: no hours listed, no contact form, not mobile-friendly, old photos?
"I noticed your shop has 47 Google reviews but no website linked" "Your Facebook page looks active but I couldn't find a website for your business" "I was looking for [service] in [city] and found you on Yelp with great reviews but no website to click through to"

Email Templates

Day 1, First Contact

These are outlines, not scripts. Write your own sentences for each part, in your own words, based on what you actually found. They follow Problem-Agitate-Solve, the structure that gets the highest reply rates on a first cold touch because it earns relevance before it asks for anything: name something real and specific (Problem), point out what that likely means for them, only if you can back it up (Agitate), then offer the fix and one soft question (Solve). Use your own judgment on Agitate, only state something as fact if you actually looked into it, otherwise soften it to "might be" or skip it. Under 100 words total. Never two questions.

Every email ends with the unsubscribe line ("To stop receiving these emails, reply with 'unsubscribe'"). That's this business's only way to opt out, required by CAN-SPAM on every commercial email. Never shorten it, reword it, or cut it to save space.

Template A, No website at all

Use when: nothing comes up when you search their name.

Subject: something short, mentions the business by name Problem: say where you found them and that you noticed no website. Name one real detail about them (review count, a service they list, how long they've been open). Agitate: what that likely means for them, e.g. people searching for what they do are probably finding a competitor's website first instead. Only say it if you'd stand behind it, soften with "probably" or "likely" rather than stating it as fact. Solve: one line on what WisdomLayers does, simple fast websites for local NC businesses. No price, that's a discovery call topic, not a first email topic. Then one soft question, would they be open to seeing what that could look like for their business. [Your name] WisdomLayers wisdomlayers.com To stop receiving these emails, reply with "unsubscribe"
Template B, Outdated website

Use when: they have a site, but you can point to one concrete thing wrong with it, something you actually saw, not a guess.

Subject: something short, references their website specifically Problem: say you were looking at businesses like theirs and came across them. Name the one specific thing you saw wrong (not mobile-friendly, broken contact form, looks dated). Agitate: what that likely means for them, e.g. visitors landing on a broken or dated page probably leave before ever calling. Only say it if you'd stand behind it, soften with "probably" or "likely" rather than stating it as fact. Solve: one line on what WisdomLayers does, helping local NC businesses modernize their site. No price, that's a discovery call topic, not a first email topic. Then one soft question, open to seeing an example of a modernized version. [Your name] WisdomLayers wisdomlayers.com To stop receiving these emails, reply with "unsubscribe"
Template C, Facebook page only

Use when: they're active on Facebook (real, recent posts) but have no website anywhere. Reference something specific from their page, not just "I found you on Facebook."

Subject: something short, mentions the business by name Problem: say you found them on Facebook and name one real detail from their page (a recent post, photos, a comment thread). No website anywhere else. Agitate: what that likely means for them, e.g. people who find them on Google or Yelp have nowhere to click through to, and a lot of customers look for a website before they call. Only say it if you'd stand behind it, soften with "probably" or "likely" rather than stating it as fact. Solve: one line on what WisdomLayers does, simple websites for NC service businesses. No price, that's a discovery call topic, not a first email topic. Then one soft question, worth a quick look at what that could look like for them. [Your name] WisdomLayers wisdomlayers.com To stop receiving these emails, reply with "unsubscribe"

Not Interested
Follow-up 1, Day 4, no reply

Reply on the same thread as your first email (hit Reply, not Compose), don't just resend it or "bump" it, that gets ignored and it's a wasted touch. Add one new piece of value that wasn't in the first email, a second detail about their business you noticed, an example site, or a different angle on the problem. Close with the same kind of easy one-line ask.

Reply on the same thread as email 1, subject line stays whatever it already is. Short, casual, like following up with someone you already emailed. New detail or new angle, not a repeat of email 1. Same single soft question at the end.
Follow-up 2, Day 9, still no reply, final

Lower the ask, this is the last touch, so make it easy to ignore with zero guilt while still leaving the door open. A concrete example (a link to a site you built) works better than another pitch.

Reply on the same thread as email 1, subject line stays whatever it already is. Say this is the last one from you. Point to something concrete, a portfolio link or example site, rather than asking again. Make clear there's no pressure and this is the end of the sequence.

After this, move them to Not Interested in the tracker. Do not contact again.

If they're interested or asking questions, a lead just went warm

Speed matters more than wording here, reply within the hour if you can. A same-day reply to a warm lead converts far better than one that sits overnight, the window closes fast once someone's attention moves on.

  1. Update tracker status to Warm Lead
  2. Notify the owner immediately, before you even finish replying
  3. Reply in your own words, short, like a text to someone you know, not a script
  4. Actually answer whatever they asked, don't dodge into a pitch
  5. Do NOT pitch hard or discuss specific pricing, that's the owner's call on the discovery call
  6. Tell them the owner will follow up, and roughly when
If they say not interested
"No worries at all, appreciate you taking the time to respond. Best of luck with the business."
If they say unsubscribe or remove me
"Of course, removing you right now. Sorry for the interruption."

Update tracker to Unsubscribed immediately. Never contact this person or business again, this is federal law, not optional.
