Email Scripts

Email Scripts and Outreach Playbook

Frameworks, not scripts. Personalize every email with something real and specific about that business. Generic emails get ignored.

Rules, Read This First

Never skip the CAN-SPAM footer, and never re-contact anyone who unsubscribed. Every email needs "To stop receiving these emails, reply with unsubscribe," and it's a legal requirement, not a style choice. Once someone unsubscribes, that's final, permanently.

Required

  • Reference something real and specific about that business
  • Verify every fact before mentioning it, never guess or invent details
  • Keep every email under 150 words
  • End with exactly one question
  • Log every business you research, even ones you skip
  • Notify the owner immediately when a lead becomes a Warm Lead
  • Reply to warm leads within one hour whenever possible

Never

  • Use the same email word-for-word on multiple businesses
  • Send from your personal email address
  • Promise pricing, turnaround times, or specific results
  • Pressure, argue with, or repeatedly contact someone who isn't interested
  • Contact anyone who unsubscribed, ever again
  • Send more than 40 emails per day per inbox
  • Skip the CAN-SPAM footer

Golden rule

Every email should feel like it was written specifically for that business. If the business owner could replace their business name with another and the email would still make sense, it isn't personalized enough.

Research Every Business First

Do not write an email until you've researched the business. Your goal is to find one or more genuine observations that make the email feel like it was written specifically for them, generic outreach gets ignored. If you can't find anything meaningful after researching, skip the lead and move on.

Research checklist, answer before writing

  1. What products or services does the business offer?
  2. Does the business have a website?
  3. If they have a website, is there one clear issue worth mentioning?
  4. Are they listed on Google Maps? How many reviews, and what's their average rating?
  5. Are they active on Facebook? Do they have a Facebook page but no website?
  6. Can you find the owner's name?
  7. Anything else worth mentioning: missing business hours, no contact form, poor mobile experience, outdated design, old project photos, broken links, missing online booking, no clear call to action?

What makes a good observation

Factual, specific, and easy to verify.

"I noticed your business has 47 Google reviews, but I couldn't find a website." "Your Facebook page is active, but I couldn't find a website linked anywhere." "I found your business while looking for plumbers in Fayetteville. Your Google reviews are excellent, but there isn't a website for customers to learn more." "Your website looks great on desktop, but the contact form doesn't appear to be working." "I noticed your gallery hasn't been updated in several years."

What to avoid. Don't make assumptions, exaggerate, or invent details. Don't mention an issue unless you've personally verified it. Never criticize the business, your observation should feel helpful, not judgmental.

Skip the lead if

  • You cannot verify key information
  • You cannot find a meaningful way to personalize the email
  • The business has recently requested not to be contacted
  • The business is permanently closed
  • The business already has a modern, well-maintained website with no obvious opportunity to mention

Quality beats quantity. A smaller list of well-researched businesses will consistently outperform a larger list of generic outreach.

Email Templates

Day 1, First Contact

These are outlines, not scripts. Write your own sentences for each part, in your own words, based on what you actually found. They follow Problem-Impact-Solution-Question, the structure that gets the highest reply rates on a first cold touch because it earns relevance before it asks for anything. Business owners skim, so keep it under 150 words total. Never two questions.

Every email ends with the unsubscribe line ("To stop receiving these emails, reply with 'unsubscribe'"). That's this business's only way to opt out, required by CAN-SPAM on every commercial email. Never shorten it, reword it, or cut it to save space.

Use when: nothing comes up when you search their name after checking Google, Facebook, Yelp, and other common listings.

After this, move them to Not Interested in the tracker. Do not contact again.

If they reply politely but aren't ready yet

A short, polite reply is a good sign, but it's not a warm lead yet. This is a Positive Response (tracked as Replied in the tool): interested enough not to ignore you, not ready for the owner.

"Thanks." "We'll keep you in mind." "Maybe later."

Update tracker status to Replied. No handoff needed yet, just log it, a real question or expressed interest is what moves them to Warm Lead.

If they're interested or asking questions, a lead just went warm

Speed matters more than wording here. Reply within one hour whenever possible, fast responses consistently outperform delayed ones.

  1. Update tracker status to Warm Lead
  2. Notify the owner immediately, before you even finish replying
  3. Reply in your own words, short, like a text to someone you know, not a script
  4. Actually answer whatever they asked, don't dodge into a pitch
  5. Do NOT pitch hard or discuss specific pricing, that's the owner's call on the discovery call
  6. Tell them the owner will follow up, and roughly when
If they say not interested
"No worries at all, appreciate you taking the time to respond. Best of luck with the business."
If they say unsubscribe or remove me
"Of course, removing you right now. Sorry for the interruption."

Update tracker to Unsubscribed immediately. Never contact this person or business again, this is federal law, not optional.

Sending Schedule

Ramp up gradually

A new email inbox needs time to build trust with email providers. Sending too many emails too soon increases the chance of messages landing in spam. Follow this schedule for every new inbox, even with hundreds of leads ready to contact.

PhaseEmails / Day / InboxPrimary Goal
Week 1, warm up5-10Establish a positive sender reputation
Week 2, warm up10-15Continue building trust, monitor deliverability
Week 3, live outreach15-20Begin generating consistent replies while maintaining inbox health
Week 4 and beyond25-30Maintain a steady pipeline without sacrificing deliverability

Daily best practices

  • Spread emails throughout the day instead of sending them all at once
  • Personalize every email before sending
  • Reply to interested businesses as quickly as possible
  • Monitor bounce rates and spam complaints daily
  • Pause outreach immediately if deliverability drops significantly, then investigate before resuming

Hard limits. Never send more than 40 cold emails per inbox per day. Never sacrifice personalization to increase volume. Every email must meet the quality standards in this playbook before it's sent.

Priority

Quality always comes before quantity. A personalized email sent to the right business is worth more than dozens of generic emails that get ignored or marked as spam.
